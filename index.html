About

SDAD focuses on one practical question: how do we make autonomous systems not only capable of driving, but capable of driving defensively under uncertainty, long-tail risks, and adversarial environments. While many workshops emphasize perception and benchmark accuracy, SDAD centers on risk-aware decision making and closed-loop safety behavior.

The workshop combines invited talks, peer-reviewed papers, benchmark challenges, and an industry panel to bridge academic innovation and real deployment constraints.

Core Questions

  • How can we model hidden intent and rare hazards early?
  • Which training paradigms improve defensive maneuvers in near-miss scenarios?
  • How should we evaluate comfort-safety trade-offs and legal compliance together?

What Makes SDAD Different

  • Defensive-driving-first benchmark and scoring protocol
  • Scenario stress tests with weather and behavior shifts
  • Joint focus on explainability, accountability, and safety

Expected Outcomes

  • Community baselines for defensive closed-loop driving
  • Shared hazard taxonomy for reproducible evaluations
  • Best-practice recommendations for deployment-oriented research

Challenges

SDAD challenge tracks are designed around defensive behavior under realistic uncertainty. Challenges are hosted by independent teams and follow transparent metric protocols.

Track 1: Hazard Anticipation

Forecast potential conflicts 2 to 5 seconds ahead in dense urban scenes with noisy observations.

  • Metrics: time-to-hazard AUC, false-alarm rate, calibration
  • Focus: intent prediction and uncertainty calibration

Track 2: Defensive Planning

Generate safe and smooth trajectories under aggressive neighboring agent behaviors.

  • Metrics: collision rate, rule compliance, comfort score
  • Focus: safety-efficiency balance in long-tail events

Track 3: Robust Closed-Loop Evaluation

Evaluate model resilience under weather shift, sensor dropout, and map perturbations.

  • Metrics: robustness index, intervention rate, recovery time
  • Focus: stability and graceful degradation under stress

Challenge details, baseline code, and submission portals will be linked here as they are released.

Submission Guidelines

  • Maximum 8 pages, excluding references.
  • Double-blind review policy is strictly enforced.
  • Supplementary materials are encouraged.
  • Accepted papers will be presented as posters or talks.

Topics of Interest

  • Defensive policy learning for autonomous driving
  • Hazard anticipation and risk-sensitive prediction
  • Uncertainty-aware planning and control
  • Long-tail scenario mining and active data curation
  • Simulation-to-real transfer for safety-critical policies
  • Adversarial robustness and failure mode analysis
  • Safety verification, formal methods, and interpretability
  • Human-centric comfort, trust, and interaction modeling
  • Vision-language driving agents with defensive priors
  • Benchmarks and reproducible safety evaluations
